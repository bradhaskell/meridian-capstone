# Bristol Farms Quietly Outshines Erewhon Across Los Angeles

Source: `raw/hoodline-bristol-farms-erewhon-2026.md`

## Summary

A Hoodline article (March 2026) comparing two premium LA-area grocery chains: Bristol Farms (13 stores, 14th opening in Hollywood) and Erewhon. Bristol Farms is positioned as the more accessible, value-relative-to-quality alternative — heavy prepared-food operations (scratch kitchens, sushi counters, cafés) at lower price points than Erewhon, versus Erewhon's exclusivity/celebrity-driven luxury branding. Directly relevant to Meridian's own competitive positioning as a smaller specialty chain competing on prepared foods and local sourcing rather than scale.

## Key points

- Bristol Farms: founded 1982 on the Palos Verdes Peninsula, 13 stores, 14th planned for Hollywood (mid-April), targeting theatergoers/tourists/Hollywood Boulevard traffic (## intro)
- Combines upscale groceries with substantial prepared-food operations: scratch kitchens, sushi counters, smoked meats, bakeries, floral, seasonal juices, in-store cafés; parent Good Food Holdings also owns Lazy Acres (## para 2)
- Price positioning below Erewhon: $4 six-ounce chocolate cookie, smoothies/juices from ~$9, while still emphasizing restaurant-style quality and made-in-store prep (## para 3)
- Strategic contrast: Erewhon relies on exclusivity, celebrity association, and luxury branding; Bristol Farms emphasizes convenience, variety, and a full-service grocery experience, expanding across affluent SoCal suburbs rather than one curated premium image (## para 4)
- Bristol Farms is explicitly seeking a "middle position" — elevated food/specialty groceries without Erewhon's price premium; success depends on marketing execution and whether customers favor the lower-priced proposition (## para 5)
