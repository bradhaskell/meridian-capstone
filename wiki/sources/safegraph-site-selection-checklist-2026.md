# Retail Site Selection Checklist: 10 Steps for Choosing a Winning Location in 2026

Source: `raw/safegraph-site-selection-checklist-2026.md`

## Summary

A SafeGraph industry guide (updated August 2026) laying out a 10-step framework for evaluating a new retail location: existing-network mapping, competitive/co-tenant effects, customer-profile matching, physical site conditions, financial/legal feasibility, current-store performance analysis, AI-assisted scoring, footprint sizing, vertical-specific weighting, and post-opening validation. Directly relevant to Meridian's Pasadena decision — Dana wants the data to back up a location call, and this framework is exactly the kind of structured site-selection process that would produce it.

## Key points

- Recommends combining internal performance records with demographic, mobility, competitive, physical, financial, legal, and post-opening data — AI can accelerate screening but doesn't replace judgment or site visits (## Main argument)
- Step 3 (customer-profile matching): population totals alone are insufficient — a smaller market with the right customer composition can outperform a denser but poorly-matched one (## 3. Match locations to the actual customer profile)
- Step 9 (vertical-specific weighting) calls out grocery specifically: use compact residential trade areas, emphasize parking/circulation/basket-oriented access and competitive saturation — different priorities than QSR or mall retail (## 9. Weight criteria by retail vertical)
- Step 10: validate the forecast at ~90 days and again at 12 months against actual visits, revenue, trade-area capture, and customer origins — treats post-opening measurement as the feedback loop that tests whether the selection process actually predicts performance (## 10. Validate the forecast after opening)
- Cited 2026 market context: U.S. shopping-center vacancy 5.9% in Q1 2026; ~5,500 openings vs. ~7,900 closures projected for 2026; e-commerce only 16.9% of U.S. retail sales in Q1 2026 (## Main argument)
