# New Grocery Formats, Store Growth and Investment Activity Signal a Sector in Motion

Source: https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/new-grocery-formats-store-growth-and-investment-signal-sector-in-motion

**Published:** April 24, 2026
**By:** John Egan, Contributor, *Commerce + Communities Today*

## Overview

The U.S. grocery market—estimated by IBISWorld at **$912.4 billion**—is undergoing significant change. Retailers are adjusting store sizes, layouts, brand positioning and customer experiences in response to changing shopping habits. Strategies range from Target's move toward a more distinctive, specialty-oriented food offering to Gelson's small "premium convenience stores" paired with electric-vehicle charging hubs.

## Grocery-anchor performance is diverging

JLL president of Americas retail advisory services **Naveen Jaggi** said traditional grocers still account for most grocery visits, but value-focused and fresh-format operators are expanding their influence. He described grocery-anchored centers as strong performers, with vacancy below the broader shopping-center market and rent premiums continuing.

JLL Income Property Trust's **Supermarket Trade Area Scoring System (STARS)** tracks approximately **40,000 grocery-anchored retail centers** nationwide. Jaggi cautioned that retailers are no longer equally effective as shopping-center anchors: **"Not all grocery anchors are created equal."**

## Specialty grocers gain share

According to **Consumer Edge's U.S. Grocery Outlook 2026**, **Trader Joe's** recorded **3% year-over-year growth** for the period ending February 28. The company outperformed the overall grocery sector by **6 percentage points**, with especially strong growth among **Gen Z shoppers**, where gains were in the double digits.

Traditional chains, including **Publix** and **Safeway**, experienced declines among every income group, with the largest pullbacks among lower-income shoppers. Growth among discount grocers such as **Aldi** and **Food 4 Less** had leveled off since mid-2025.

Consumer Edge senior vice president of research and market intelligence **Michael Gunther** said shoppers are choosing retailers based not only on price but also on a clearly differentiated reason to remain loyal, including hard-discount value or specialty private-label products. He said conventional supermarkets are being squeezed between those two positions.

## Target emphasizes differentiation and experience

Target is reshaping its grocery strategy as specialty grocers gain traction. Its food-and-beverage category produced more than **$24 billion in fiscal 2025 net sales**; the fiscal year ended February 1.

Executive vice president and chief marketing officer **Cara Sylvester** said Target does not intend to become a conventional, full-line grocer. Instead, the company is emphasizing emerging brands, wellness products and owned brands, with an experience designed for families seeking distinctive and trend-oriented food choices.

Target is also increasing investment in the in-store food experience, including food sampling. Sylvester said the goal is for shopping for food at Target to feel **"distinctly Target."**

## Walmart and Costco expand and reinvest in stores

**Walmart** is remodeling more than **650 standard stores and Neighborhood Markets** during 2026 and early 2027, while planning roughly **20 new stores** during that period. In fiscal 2026, which ended January 31, the company spent more than **$5.5 billion** on approximately **675 remodels** and opened **12 stores**. Walmart had more than **5,200 U.S. stores** as of January 31.

**Costco** expects to open more than **30 locations annually** in coming years. Its fiscal 2026 plan calls for **28 net new stores**; that fiscal year began September 1. Costco operates nearly **640 U.S. warehouses**.

## Aldi pilots a flexible layout

**Aldi South Group** is testing a new global store design in the United States. The pilot began in September in **Aventura, Florida**, near Miami, and is expected to continue elsewhere in Florida through 2026.

The format uses modular components that can fit different store sizes, from suburban supermarkets to smaller urban locations. The approach is intended to accommodate varying real-estate conditions and shopping patterns while preserving Aldi's emphasis on efficiency, low staffing and cost control.

Aldi South Group operated nearly **7,600 stores across the United States and 10 other countries** as of 2024, including more than **2,400 U.S. locations**. To celebrate its **50th anniversary in the United States**, Aldi plans to open **150 U.S. stores in 2026**.

## Grocery-anchored investment remains substantial

U.S. transactions involving grocery-anchored centers totaled nearly **$11 billion in 2025**, a **42% increase from 2024**, according to **JLL's Grocery Tracker 2026**.

Recent examples include:

- **Brixton Capital** acquired two grocery-anchored centers for nearly **$130 million**.
- Its purchase of **Las Palmas Village** in Las Vegas cost **$29.4 million**. The **106,838-square-foot**, **Vons-anchored** property was **95% leased**.
- Brixton earlier bought the **Vallarta Supermarkets-anchored Quad at Whittier** in suburban Los Angeles for **$100 million**. The nine-building, **314,593-square-foot** center was **95.6% leased**.
- **Medipower** purchased a **558,000-square-foot portfolio of seven grocery-anchored centers** for **$115 million**.
- **Nuveen** paid **$298 million** for a **956,865-square-foot, seven-property portfolio**, six of whose centers were grocery-anchored.
- During 2025 and 2026, **Blackstone Real Estate** acquired three primarily grocery-anchored portfolios totaling **130 properties** for **$6.7 billion**. **Kroger** is among the tenant brands represented.

Despite a broad slowdown in retail construction, development continues in select markets. **Regency Centers** is building **The Marketplace**, a **71,400-square-foot Whole Foods-anchored center in Redlands, California**. The Whole Foods store will occupy **36,000 square feet**. Four additional buildings will provide space for up to **18 retailers**, and the project also includes a drive-through pad site.

## Grocery and convenience stores compete with restaurants

The **2026 Phygital Index Report** from **Tillster**, based on a survey of **2,144 U.S. diners**, found reduced visits to restaurant chains:

- **29%** said they were visiting fast-food restaurants less often.
- **37%** reported fewer visits to fast-casual restaurants.
- **36%** said they were visiting grocery stores more frequently.
- **33%** said they were going to convenience stores more often.

Tillster concluded that grocery and convenience operators are capturing dining occasions by competing on perceived value. The company said restaurants need to improve personalized and seamless experiences rather than rely solely on discounts. Tillster provides commerce technology to quick-service and fast-casual brands.

## Gelson's combines small-format grocery with EV charging

The **Food Industry Association (FMI)** reports that U.S. grocery stores are generally becoming smaller. Southern California grocer **Gelson's**, which operates **27 traditional supermarkets**, is pursuing an especially compact model.

Its second **ReCharge by Gelson's** location recently opened in **Costa Mesa** and measures **5,400 square feet**. The first, in nearby **Santa Ana**, measures **3,000 square feet**.

These stores are positioned as **"premium convenience stores"** and are paired with **Rove** fast-charging hubs for electric vehicles. Amenities include:

- Indoor seating and lounge space
- Free Wi-Fi
- Restrooms
- A car wash
- Fresh produce, bread, beverages, snacks, pasta, beer and wine
- Prepared food such as breakfast burritos, chicken wings and pizza
- Grab-and-go sandwiches and small meals

Overall, the article portrays grocery retail as a sector where specialty positioning, flexible footprints, physical-store investment, grocery-anchored real estate and foodservice competition are all intensifying.
